Overview

1. Project Objectives & Problem Statement

Energy pricing is one of the most critical determinants of modern macroeconomic stability, direct consumer inflation, and industrial logistics. While upstream crude oil is priced in standardized global commodity benchmarks (such as Brent Crude), retail prices at domestic fueling pumps vary by more than 1000% from one nation to another.

The primary objective of Global Fuel Prices and Taxation Trends is to investigate and visually decouple the fundamental forces driving these retail disparities:

  • Fiscal Wedge: Measuring how sovereign tax percentages and excise duties inflate consumer pump prices.
  • Subsidies & Energy Sovereignty: Evaluating how government subsidy tiers cushion consumers in oil-rich vs. energy-importing economies.
  • Socioeconomic Affordability: Contrasting fuel burden across World Bank income levels (High, Upper Middle, Lower Middle, and Low Income).
  • Temporal Commodity Correlation: Assessing the degree and speed of Brent Crude international price pass-through to retail Petrol, Diesel, and LPG products.

Data Engineering

3. Data Preparation & Preprocessing Methodology

Currency & Unit Harmonization

Retail prices originally quoted in local sovereign currencies and domestic measurement units (e.g., US Gallons vs Imperial Liters) were converted to standardized US Dollars per Liter (USD/L) using historical monthly foreign exchange averages.

Temporal Normalization

Observation dates were synchronized across multi-source reporting registries. Irregular temporal intervals were aggregated to common periodic timestamps to enable aligned longitudinal joins with monthly Brent Crude benchmark averages.

Missing Value & Outlier Remediation

Missing values in LPG or Diesel records for small island states were inspected. Extreme reporting errors or hyperinflation artifacts were verified against secondary registries or excluded from comparative percentiles to preserve statistical integrity.

Categorical Standardization

National Income Levels were harmonized with current World Bank Atlas method definitions. Regional boundaries were mapped to official UN geographic classifications to enable hierarchically consistent drill-downs.

Analytical Boundaries

6. Limitations of the Analysis

Methodological Constraints & Data Caveats
  • Foreign Exchange Volatility: Standardizing retail fuel prices in USD exposes historical trends to exchange rate swings, which can amplify or diminish local currency price stability.
  • Reporting Lag: Developing economies often report fuel statistics on varying quarterly cycles compared to daily/weekly reporting in OECD member nations.
  • Sub-National Variance: State-level or provincial fuel tax variations (such as distinct US state gas taxes or Canadian provincial fuel taxes) are aggregated to national averages.
  • Fuel Quality & Octane Differences: Regular 87/91 AKI gasoline and premium blends are harmonized under generic petrol classifications.
